My career spans rigorous academic research and production AI in industry. I lead AI engineering projects on a data analytics and insights team, building agentic systems, causal inference pipelines and forecasting frameworks on Databricks, Azure and Google Cloud.

Research

My research is about AI that can reason about cause and effect, coordinate as teams of agents, and hold up in real industrial settings. It runs from my doctoral work on multi-agent coordination to applied AI research I lead in industry today, with results in peer-reviewed venues and patents.

Current research themes

Reliable agentic AI and multi-agent coordination

Industry research · 2024 – Present

How to make teams of LLM agents dependable enough for production: planner and specialist-agent designs, critic and validator loops that check answers before people see them, and human review at the decision point. This extends my earlier work on coordination protocols for autonomous vehicles to modern language-model agents.

LLM agentsOrchestrationValidationHuman in the loop

Causal AI for industrial decision-making

Industry research · 2022 – Present

Using causal discovery and causal inference so models explain why outcomes change, not only predict them. The work pairs causal models with SHAP and LLM-generated explanations so business users can question and trust the results.

Causal discoveryCausal inferenceExplainable AI

Root-cause analysis and anomaly detection for industrial processes

Industry research · 2023 – Present · 5 patents · Frontiers in Manufacturing Technology, 2024

Golden-batch methods that compare each production run against an ideal reference to find what went wrong and why, plus batch synchronization with dynamic time warping and anomaly detection for processes such as bioreactor fermentation.

Root-cause analysisDynamic time warpingAnomaly detection

Interpretable forecasting with wavelet-enhanced ensembles

Industry research · 2022 – Present · Innovation Award

Combining wavelet signal decomposition with ensemble machine learning and causally selected drivers, so forecasts are both more accurate and easy for leaders to interpret.

Time seriesWaveletsEnsemble learning

Developed path planning algorithms for the drones NOAA sends into hurricanes, using information-theoretic criteria to target the observations that most improve predictions of storm path and intensity. Covered in national news and by NC A&T.
